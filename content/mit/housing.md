Housing at MIT is famously culture-driven: dorms have strong, distinct personalities, and students pick where they live. (Specifics checked Aug 2026.)

## The undergrad dorms
**Dining-hall dorms** (meal plan required): **Baker** (Alvar Aalto building, social), **Maseeh** (largest, closest to classes), **New Vassar** (newest, opened 2021, maker spaces), **Next House** (big west-campus community), **Simmons** (the "sponge" architectural landmark), and **McCormick** (all-women) — though note **McCormick closed in summer 2026 for a two-year renovation** (reopening ~2028; women-only floors set up in Tang for displaced residents who want them).

**Cook-for-yourself dorms** (kitchens, no meal plan required): **Burton Conner** (floor-based cooking culture, renovated 2022), **East Campus** (DIY culture, courtyard builds during REX), **MacGregor** (singles for everyone, entry-based), **New House** (includes cultural houses: Chocolate City, La Casa, French House, German House, iHouse), and **Random Hall** (small, quirky, near Central Square).

The dining vs. cook-for-yourself split meaningfully affects cost and lifestyle.

## How you get housed (first-year process)
1. **Summer**: housing application → summer lottery gives you an initial dorm assignment (rank dorms using the Guide to Residences and dorm videos).
2. **REX (Residential Exploration)** during orientation (Aug 29–Sep 2 in 2026): dorms throw events so you can feel out cultures.
3. **FYRE (First-Year Residence Exchange)**: the adjustment lottery running alongside REX — apply if you want to switch dorms; if you get a new assignment, you move.
4. In-house room assignment follows, run by each dorm (often very student-controlled: floors/entries pick their people).
- **All first-years live on campus.** Housing is guaranteed for **eight consecutive semesters** (four years) as long as you file the annual Housing Intent form; most undergrads stay on campus all four years.
- Returning students: in-dorm retention first, cross-dorm transfer lottery if switching.

## Cost
Rates are tiered by dorm (2025–26, per term): roughly **$5,800–$7,500** — e.g. Tier 1 doubles ~$6,800/singles ~$7,500 (Baker, EC, Maseeh, New Vassar, Simmons...); Random Hall is the cheapest tier. Current rates: mitguidetoresidences.mit.edu → Billing and Fees.

## FSILGs (Fraternities, Sororities, and Independent Living Groups)
~44 FSILGs (about 30 fraternities, 9 sororities, 5 ILGs), many with houses in Boston/Cambridge; roughly a third of undergrads affiliate. First-years live in dorms but can rush/join immediately; members typically move in sophomore year. **ILGs** (co-ed independent houses like tEp, pika, Student House, WILG) are a distinct MIT institution — quirky, self-governed, often cheaper. IFC rush and Panhel recruitment happen in September (sorority recruitment is formal; fraternity/ILG rush is a free-food-events sprint).

## Grad housing (for reference)
Grad residences: Ashdown, Sidney-Pacific, Tang, Edgerton, Westgate (family), The Warehouse, Graduate Tower at Site 4, and 70 Amherst St — separate application, not guaranteed.
