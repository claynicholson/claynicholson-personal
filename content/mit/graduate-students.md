MIT has more grad students (~7,000+) than undergrads. The systems differ substantially:

## Structure and funding
- Admissions and requirements are **entirely departmental** — each department sets quals, milestones, and funding norms.
- PhD students are typically fully funded via **RA (research assistantship)**, **TA**, or fellowship; stipend levels are set annually. Grad students at MIT voted to unionize (**MIT GSU/UE**) — stipends/benefits are covered by the collective bargaining agreement; see the union and OGE sites for current terms.
- Master's programs vary: some funded (course-6 MEng for MIT undergrads), many self/employer-funded (Sloan, SDM, etc.).

## Key offices
- **OGE (Office of Graduate Education)** — policies, fellowships, and **GradSupport** (the grad version of S³: confidential deans for advisor conflicts, personal crises, academic trouble).
- **GSC (Graduate Student Council)** — student government, funding for grad groups, advocacy.
- **iREFS / REFS** — peer conflict-support resources in many departments.

## Living
- **Grad housing** (Sidney-Pacific, Ashdown, Westgate, Tang, Edgerton, Warehouse, Grad Tower at Site 4) via annual lottery — not guaranteed; many grads live in Cambridge/Somerville/Boston. Family housing exists (Westgate/Eastgate legacy).
- Most other resources (Z Center, libraries, MIT Medical, transportation, clubs) serve grads identically; grad-specific social life runs through dorms' events, the GSC, the Muddy Charles Pub (the campus grad pub in Walker), and department culture.

## Academic life notes
- Advisor choice matters more than anything; GradSupport and REFS exist precisely because of that.
- Cross-registration (Harvard) and the same libraries/IS&T/software resources apply.
- The **thesis** goes through department + Institute formatting rules; MIT theses are published openly in DSpace.
