MIT has more grad students (~7,000+) than undergrads. The systems differ substantially:

## Structure and funding
- Admissions and requirements are **entirely departmental** — each department sets quals, milestones, and funding norms.
- PhD students are typically fully funded via **RA (research assistantship)**, **TA**, or fellowship. 2025–26 base rates: RA doctoral **$4,269/mo (~$51.2k/yr)**, TA **$4,369/mo (~$52.4k/yr)**, RA master's ~$3,899/mo. Grad students are unionized (**MIT GSU/UE**); the 2023–2026 contract expired May 2026 and negotiations were ongoing as of mid-2026, so check oge.mit.edu/stipend-rates and mitgsu.org for current numbers.
- Master's programs vary: some funded (course-6 MEng for MIT undergrads), many self/employer-funded (Sloan, SDM, etc.).
- **Time off** (per the union contract): RAs/TAs accrue 5 vacation days per appointment period (max 15/yr) plus Institute holidays and winter break relief. **Childbirth accommodation** gives birth parents up to two months' relief; parental accommodation covers other new parents (apply to OGE 3–4 months ahead).
- **Families**: the MIT Grant for Graduate Students with Children (need-based, up to ~$10k), subsidized backup childcare ($5/hr in-home, $10/day in-center), and family housing at Westgate.

## Key offices
- **OGE (Office of Graduate Education)** — policies, fellowships, and **GradSupport** (the grad version of S³: confidential deans for advisor conflicts, personal crises, academic trouble).
- **GSC (Graduate Student Council)** — student government, funding for grad groups, advocacy.
- **iREFS / REFS** — peer conflict-support resources in many departments.

## Living
- **Grad housing** (Sidney-Pacific, Ashdown, Tang, Edgerton, Westgate for families, The Warehouse, Graduate Tower at Site 4, 70 Amherst St): you pick your exact unit via **self-selection in the Housing Portal**; rent includes utilities with no deposit (+$5–10/mo house tax). Recent efficiencies ran roughly $1,700–$2,400/mo depending on building; rates at graduatehousing.mit.edu. Not guaranteed — many grads live in Cambridge/Somerville/Boston (sublicensecenter.mit.edu helps).
- Most other resources (Z Center, libraries, MIT Health, transportation, clubs) serve grads identically; grad-specific social life runs through dorm events, the GSC, the **Muddy Charles Pub** (Walker Memorial, open weekday afternoons/evenings, MIT ID + 21+), and department culture.

## Academic life notes
- Advisor choice matters more than anything; GradSupport and REFS exist precisely because of that.
- Cross-registration (Harvard) and the same libraries/IS&T/software resources apply.
- The **thesis** goes through department + Institute formatting rules and hard Registrar deadlines per degree cycle (Sept degrees ~early Aug, Feb degrees ~mid-Jan, spring degrees in May; $85 late-title fee); MIT theses are published openly in DSpace.
- **GSC money**: conference grants (registration/travel/lodging your advisor won't cover) and professional-development grants — gsc.mit.edu/funding.
