## CAPD (Career Advising & Professional Development)
The career office: resume/cover letter reviews, mock interviews, career advising by field, pre-health and pre-law advising, grad school application help. Free and underused — book appointments via Handshake.

## The recruiting calendar
- **Fall Career Fair** (September, student-run, one of the country's largest) — hundreds of companies; bring printed resumes. Companies also run info sessions ("free dinner + pitch") all fall.
- **Tech/software/finance/quant recruiting** starts absurdly early — big-company software and quant internship apps open late summer/early fall for the *following* summer. Sophomore/junior summer internships are the norm; first-years target smaller companies, research, or programs like MISTI.
- **xFair** (spring, TechX) and startup-focused fairs round out the year.
- **Handshake** is MIT's job board; many roles also come via department mailing lists and club pipelines (e.g., trading clubs, consulting clubs).

## IAP Externships
Month-long January placements with alumni-hosted companies — apply through CAPD in early fall (lottery/selection). Low-commitment way to try an industry.

## Common paths and resources
- **UROP → grad school**: research + rec letters; CAPD and departments run PhD application panels. GRE increasingly optional.
- **Pre-med**: prehealth advising through CAPD (committee letters, timeline planning).
- **International students**: CPT/OPT work authorization goes through the ISO — start paperwork early (see the international-students topic).
- Salary/outcome data: MIT publishes graduating-student survey data (capd.mit.edu) — useful for negotiating.

## Practical norms
- MIT resume style: one page, GPA on /5.0 scale (list it as X.X/5.0), course numbers are fine to list but spell out names for non-MIT readers.
- Referrals via upperclassmen/alumni move applications more than career-fair resume drops; the alumni advisors hub connects students with alumni.
