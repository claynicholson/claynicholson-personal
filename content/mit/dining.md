## Dining halls and meal plans
Five-ish residential dining halls: **Maseeh (Howard Dining Hall)**, **Baker**, **McCormick**, **Next House**, **Simmons**, and **New Vassar**. All-you-care-to-eat swipes; brunch on weekends. Students living in dining dorms must buy a meal plan (tiered by swipes/week); students in cook-for-yourself dorms can optionally buy plans or pay per-meal.

## Retail dining and cafés
Campus cafés and quick options cluster in the Student Center (W20), Stata Center, Kendall/Sloan side (E62 café), and food trucks. The Student Center has late-night options; hours shift every year. **TechCASH** (stored value on your MIT ID) works at campus dining, many nearby restaurants, laundry, and printing.

## Cook-for-yourself life
EC/Random/Burton Conner/MacGregor culture includes floor cooking. Groceries: Trader Joe's and Whole Foods within walking/shuttle distance, H Mart in Central Square, Costco runs via Zipcar, and the daily **Bread and Bananas**-style mutual aid and dorm food-buying co-ops that come and go.

## The free food ecosystem
- **free-food@mit.edu mailing list** ("psst free food in 32-G882") — legendary; join it.
- Club meetings, seminars, and sponsor events feed a large fraction of campus.
- **CommonWealth Kitchen / SwipeShare / Food security resources**: MIT runs food-security programs (swipe donation, the **MIT Food Pantry** in W20) — see DoingWell/studentlife resources if money for food is tight.

## Nearby food (the classics)
Kendall Square (fancier lunch), Central Square (cheap and diverse — Falafel Palace, Little Donkey side), the Z-Center side of Mass Ave (Flour Bakery, Area Four), Chinatown and Allston via T for group dinners. Late-night delivery culture is strong; dorms have standing recommendations.
