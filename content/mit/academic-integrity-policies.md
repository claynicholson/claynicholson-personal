## Collaboration culture
MIT is unusually collaboration-friendly: working on psets together is normal and often explicitly encouraged. The universal rules:
- **Each class sets its own collaboration policy** — read the syllabus; it varies from "work together freely" to "no collaboration."
- Write up your own solutions in your own words; credit collaborators when asked.
- Copying solutions (from friends, past terms, "bibles," or the internet) presented as your own work is plagiarism.
- AI-tool policies (ChatGPT/Claude/Copilot etc.) are set per-class — many allow assisted use with disclosure, some prohibit it. When unspecified, ask.

## Exams
Closed/open-book rules per class. Cheating cases go to the **Committee on Discipline (COD)**, which handles academic and non-academic misconduct; sanctions range from grade penalties to suspension/expulsion.

## Official policy homes
- **integrity.mit.edu** — the academic integrity handbook (citation, collaboration, writing code honestly).
- **handbook.mit.edu** — the Mind and Hand Book: student conduct policies.
- **policies.mit.edu** — Institute-wide policies.

## Other policies students actually hit
- **Petitions**: late drops, requirement substitutions, and exceptions go through the Committee on Academic Performance (CAP) or your department — S³ helps undergrads with CAP petitions.
- **Hacking ethics**: MIT's exploration culture ("hacking" in the exploring-buildings-and-pranks sense) has well-known informal ethics — be safe, don't damage, don't steal — but roof-and-tunnel access without authorization is against policy; know the risk.
- **Copyright / course materials**: class materials belong to instructors — posting them publicly (e.g., to CourseHero/Chegg) violates policy.
- **Nondiscrimination, Title IX**: the Institute Discrimination & Harassment Response Office (IDHR) handles reports.
