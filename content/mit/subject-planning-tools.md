The tools basically every MIT student uses to plan classes:

## Hydrant (hydrant.mit.edu)
Student-built schedule planner (successor to Firehose). Search subjects, see meeting times, build conflict-free schedules, export to calendar. Shows units, ratings, and in-class hours from course evaluations. The default way to build your term.

## CourseRoad (courseroad.mit.edu)
Student-built four-year degree planner. Drag subjects into future terms and it checks them against your major's requirements and the GIRs — great for "can I fit a double major" questions.

## Official subject listing & catalog
- **student.mit.edu/catalog** — the full subject listing with descriptions, prereqs, units, and terms offered.
- **catalog.mit.edu** — the MIT Bulletin: degree charts, requirements, policies.
- **WebSIS (student.mit.edu)** — registration, grades, degree audit.

## Course evaluations
End-of-term subject evaluations (ratings and hours/week) are published internally; Hydrant surfaces the headline numbers. Hours ratings are the honest signal for workload.

## OpenCourseWare (ocw.mit.edu)
Free public course materials for thousands of past MIT subjects — lecture notes, psets, exams. Useful for previewing a class before taking it or reviewing prerequisites.

## Other signals
- **Underground guides / department wikis** — some departments (notably Course 6's HKN evaluations at hkn.mit.edu) publish student-written subject reviews.
- **Shopping week culture**: MIT doesn't have a formal shopping period, but attending several candidate classes before Add Date and dropping extras later is standard.
- **Units**: MIT subjects are rated in units like "5-0-7" (lecture-lab-outside work hours/week; total = credit units, typically 12). A normal load is 48–60 units (4–5 classes).
