Every MIT undergraduate, regardless of major, completes the General Institute Requirements (GIRs).

## Science Core (6 subjects)
- **Physics I** — 8.01 (mechanics), with variants 8.011, 8.012, 8.01L
- **Physics II** — 8.02 (electricity & magnetism), with variants 8.021, 8.022
- **Calculus I** — 18.01 (single variable), with variants 18.01A, 18.014
- **Calculus II** — 18.02 (multivariable), with variants 18.02A, 18.022, 18.024
- **Chemistry** — 3.091, 5.111, or 5.112
- **Biology** — 7.012, 7.013, 7.014, 7.015, or 7.016

Many students place out of some of these via Advanced Standing Exams (ASEs) or approved AP/IB/transfer credit (mainly 18.01; physics/chem/bio credit is more limited — check the official rules).

## HASS Requirement (8 subjects)
Eight Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences subjects, including:
- **Distribution**: at least one each of HASS-H (humanities), HASS-A (arts), HASS-S (social sciences)
- **Concentration**: 3–4 subjects forming a concentration in one HASS field (you file a concentration proposal with an advisor)

## Communication Requirement (4 CI subjects)
Four Communication-Intensive subjects: two CI-H/CI-HW (in HASS, usually one per year for the first two years) and two CI-M (in your major). Incoming students take a writing assessment (FEE — First-Year Essay Evaluation) that determines whether the first CI-H must be a CI-HW (writing-focused).

## REST Requirement (2 subjects)
Two subjects from the Restricted Electives in Science and Technology list — intro-level STEM subjects outside the science core (e.g., 6.100A, 18.03, 5.12, many department intros). Most majors' requirements automatically cover REST.

## Laboratory Requirement (12 units)
One Institute Lab subject (12 units) with substantial hands-on experimental work. Many majors build this in.

## Physical Education & Swim
- **8 PE points** — typically four quarter-long PE classes at 2 points each (a huge menu: sailing, archery, pistol, fencing, weight training, etc.). Registration for PE classes is a separate lottery each quarter and fills fast.
- **Swim requirement** — pass the 100-yard swim test (offered during orientation and each term) or complete a swim class.

## Practical notes
- GIR subjects are offered every year and most every term; the science core is usually best finished in the first year.
- Your degree audit (in WebSIS) tracks GIR progress automatically.
- "GIRs" is also used loosely to mean just the science core ("I'm done with my GIRs").
