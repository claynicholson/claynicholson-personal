Every MIT undergraduate, regardless of major, completes the General Institute Requirements (GIRs).

## Science Core (6 subjects)
- **Physics I** — 8.01 (mechanics), with variants 8.011, 8.012, 8.01L
- **Physics II** — 8.02 (electricity & magnetism), with variants 8.021, 8.022
- **Calculus I** — 18.01 (single variable), with variants 18.01A, 18.014
- **Calculus II** — 18.02 (multivariable), with variants 18.02A, 18.022, 18.024
- **Chemistry** — 3.091, 5.111, or 5.112
- **Biology** — 7.012, 7.013, 7.014, 7.015, or 7.016

Many students place out of some of these via **Advanced Standing Exams** (offered for 18.01/18.02, 8.01–8.04, 7.012, chemistry, and some EECS subjects — held in summer, during orientation, and in fall finals week; P/NR for first-years) or via AP credit. AP credit is narrow: **score of 5 only** — Calculus BC → 18.01 credit; both Physics C exams (Mechanics + E&M) at 5 → 8.01 credit; **no credit for AP Biology, Chemistry, Computer Science, or Physics 1/2** at any score (humanities APs at 5 earn 9 units of unrestricted elective credit each). See firstyear.mit.edu for the current sheet.

## HASS Requirement (8 subjects)
Eight Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences subjects, including:
- **Distribution**: one each of HASS-H (humanities), HASS-A (arts), HASS-S (social sciences)
- **Concentration**: 3–4 subjects forming a concentration in one HASS field (you file a concentration proposal with an advisor); one subject may double-count for distribution and concentration with advisor permission
- The remainder are HASS electives (any category, or HASS-E)

## Communication Requirement (4 CI subjects)
Four Communication-Intensive subjects: two CI-H (CI-HW is the writing-focused variant that fills a CI-H slot) and two CI-M (in your major). The required pace: **1 CI subject by end of year one, 2 by end of year two, 3 by year three, all 4 to graduate** — CAP follows up if you fall behind. Incoming students take a writing assessment (FEE — First-Year Essay Evaluation) that determines whether the first CI-H must be a CI-HW.

## REST Requirement (2 subjects)
Two subjects from the Restricted Electives in Science and Technology list — intro-level STEM subjects outside the science core (e.g., 6.100A, 18.03, 5.12, many department intros). Most majors' requirements automatically cover REST.

## Laboratory Requirement (12 units)
One Institute Lab subject (12 units) with substantial hands-on experimental work. Many majors build this in.

## Physical Education & Swim
- **8 PE points**, expected complete by the end of second year — typically four quarter-long PE classes at 2 points each (a huge menu: sailing, archery, pistol, fencing, weight training...), though two varsity seasons or ROTC also count. PE registration is a separate lottery each quarter and fills fast.
- **Swim requirement** — swim 100 continuous yards (4 pool lengths, any stroke, no time limit, first 3 lengths on your front), or pass a beginning swim class. The test is offered during orientation and each term.

## Practical notes
- **ASEs are one attempt per subject** — once you've sat one, you generally can't retake it, so take them seriously.
- GIR subjects are offered every year and most every term; the science core is usually best finished in the first year. 18.01A/8.01L variants run on shifted schedules (18.01A covers 18.01 in the first half of fall).
- Your degree audit (in WebSIS) tracks GIR progress automatically.
- "GIRs" is also used loosely to mean just the science core ("I'm done with my GIRs").
