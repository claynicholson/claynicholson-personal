MIT identifies buildings by **number**, not name — even named buildings go by number in practice.

## Decoding room numbers
`10-250` = Building 10, floor 2, room 50. `E14-633` = East campus building 14, 6th floor. Prefixes: **E** (east of Ames St), **W** (west of Mass Ave), **N/NE/NW** (north of the tracks). No prefix = main group. Basement rooms get 0-prefixed floors (66-0025).

## Landmark buildings
- **Building 7 / Lobby 7** — the Mass Ave entrance, small dome; start of the **Infinite Corridor** (251m straight shot through 7-3-10-4-8).
- **Building 10 / Great Dome** — the iconic dome over Killian Court; Barker Library beneath; hack target of legend.
- **10-250 and 26-100** — the classic large lecture halls; **Kresge (W16)** for the biggest events.
- **Stata Center (32)** — the melting Gehry building; CSAIL; contains 32-123 lecture hall; famously unnavigable.
- **Green Building (54)** — tallest in Cambridge, EAPS; radome on top; giant Tetris hack site; wind tunnel effect at its base is folklore-grade real.
- **Student Center (W20)** — food, SIPB, offices, study space, the Coop below? (the MIT Coop bookstore is in Kendall; W20 hosts services).
- **Media Lab (E14/E15)**, **Sloan (E62)**, **Koch (76)**, **Brain & Cog (46)** — the east frontier.
- **Walker Memorial (50)** — historic hall (WMBR in the basement); **Z Center (W35)** — gym/pool.
- **Killian Court** — the Great Dome lawn facing the Charles; graduation happens here.

## Tools
- **whereis.mit.edu** — official campus map (search any room number).
- **MIT Mobile/Atlas app** — maps, shuttle tracking, ID features.
- The **tunnel system** connects much of main campus underground — learn a route or two before February.

## Folklore-level facts (real)
- The Infinite Corridor aligns with the sunset ~twice a year: **MIThenge** — crowds gather.
- The Harvard Bridge (Mass Ave to Boston) is measured in **Smoots** (364.4 ± 1 ear); painted markings maintained by fraternity pledges since 1958.
- The Alchemist statue, the Ray & Maria (Stata), Transparent Horizon (the unloved black steel sculpture by EC) — public art is everywhere (List Center map).
