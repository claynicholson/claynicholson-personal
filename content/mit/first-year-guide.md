## Before you arrive
- **FPOPs (Freshman Pre-Orientation Programs)**: optional week-early programs (outdoors, engineering, service, etc.) — a great way to make early friends. Apply in the summer.
- **Advanced credit**: AP credit needs a **5** — Calc BC → 18.01, both Physics C exams → 8.01; no AP credit for bio/chem/CS/Physics 1&2 (humanities 5s earn 9 elective units each). **ASEs (Advanced Standing Exams)** — offered in summer, during orientation, and fall finals week — let you test out of science core subjects regardless of AP scores.
- **FEE (First-Year Essay Evaluation)**: online writing assessment that determines whether your first CI-H subject must be a CI-HW.
- **Math diagnostic** for physics placement; some subjects have placement/diagnostic requirements.

## Orientation (late August)
Week of events before classes: swim test, advising meetings, department fair, Activities Midway (club fair, Johnson Athletic Center), and **REX** (Residential Exploration — dorm events). You arrive with a summer-lottery dorm assignment and can switch via **FYRE** (First-Year Residence Exchange) during REX week — see the housing topic.

Also worth knowing: **first-year learning communities** — Concourse (~50 students), ESG (~55), Terrascope, and DesignPlus — offer small-cohort ways to take the first-year core; apply the summer before via the Office of the First Year.

## Advising
Every first-year gets an advisor plus an associate advisor (upperclassman) via one of a few formats (traditional advising, advising seminars). You must meet your advisor to register each term. After declaring a major you get a departmental advisor.

## Academics in year one
- **Fall and IAP are Pass/No Record**; spring is ABC/No Record (see the grading topic).
- **Credit limits**: 54 units in fall, 12 in IAP, 60 in spring (+ up to 6 units of discovery subjects each semester; PE excluded) — no overloading allowed.
- Typical first-year schedule: 3 science core subjects + 1 HASS (often the CI-H) + maybe a seminar/PE.
- **Recitations**: most core classes have large lectures plus small recitations; switching recitation sections to get a good instructor is common and allowed.
- **Psets rule everything**: collaboration is generally encouraged (write up your own work) — check each class's policy.

## Getting help early
- Office hours (go — everyone goes), TSR² tutoring (Office of Minority Education, open to all), department tutoring (e.g., HKN for Course 6, math learning center), S³ (Student Support Services) when life interferes with school.

## Common first-year advice
- Take the ASEs seriously if you have the background — skipping 18.01/8.01 saves a term slot but only skip what you truly know.
- Don't overload spring just because fall felt fine on P/NR.
- Use P/NR to explore a weird class, join clubs, and learn to sleep at a functional hour.
