MIT's grading system is deliberately forgiving early on so students can adjust.

## First year: Pass/No Record and ABC/No Record
- **Fall of first year: Pass/No Record (P/NR).** Every subject is graded P (C-level or better) or NR. An NR never appears on your external transcript — the subject simply vanishes. This is the famous "P/NR semester."
- **Spring of first year: ABC/No Record.** Grades of A, B, C appear on your transcript; D or F becomes NR (no external record). So you can take risks all year without a failing grade following you.
- First-years also have a **credit limit** (set by the Committee on Academic Performance; roughly 54 units in fall and slightly more in spring — check firstyear.mit.edu for the current numbers) to prevent overloading.

## Sophomore Exploratory
Sophomores may designate one subject per term as **Exploratory**: after seeing the final grade, you choose whether to keep the grade or convert the subject to Listener (no credit, no grade). Great for trying a hard or out-of-major class risk-free.

## Junior/Senior P/D/F
Juniors and seniors get a budget of units (historically up to 48) they can elect to take P/D/F instead of letter grades, subject to restrictions (generally not usable for major/minor requirements — check the registrar's current rules).

## Regular grading
- Letter grades: A, B, C, D, F, with internal +/- modifiers (pluses and minuses appear on internal grade reports but **not** on the external transcript or in GPA).
- MIT GPA is on a **5.0 scale** (A=5, B=4, C=3, D=2, F=0).
- **Incomplete (I)** — for missing a small amount of work for good cause, arranged with the instructor.
- **Listener (L)** — auditing; appears with no credit.

## Dropping and flexibility
- **Add Date** and **Drop Date** each term are generous (Drop Date is around three-quarters through the term) — dropping by Drop Date leaves no record. After Drop Date, late drops require petitioning.
- Many students register for extra subjects and drop later; this is normal and expected.

Always check the Registrar's site for the current year's exact policies and dates — flexible-grading rules have been adjusted several times in recent years.
